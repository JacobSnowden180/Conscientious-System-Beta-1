What it does

Real work, off your plate.

A small staff, each one focused on a part of ordinary life, able to hand work to the others. You talk to one teammate. They sort out who does what.

01 Files

Spreadsheets, documents, and Drive

It opens what you already keep, updates the sheet, and leaves the next version where you will look for it tomorrow.

02 Web

Research, options, and pages filled in

It does the looking. Three choices side by side, a form filled in, and a short note on what it found and why.

03 Messages

Drafts that wait for a yes

Replies written for you to read. You change a line or you approve it. Nothing is sent in your name until then.

04 Routines

Routines that come back

Say it once. The morning sheet, the afternoon nudge, and the Thursday thing you always forget show up again on their own.

05 A team

They coordinate with each other

Schedule, nutrition, finances, home, pets, and fitness stay on their own work, then pass the relevant part along. The kitchen hears what Thursday already promised.

06 Practice

A day you can print and review

Printable day schedules, a habit list, and a weekly look at whether the plan held. The same conscientiousness practice, run each morning.

How it works

Three moves. Then the day runs.

  1. 01

    You talk

    Type it, or place a voice call. You can be in the kitchen. The desk can stay closed.

    “Plan Thursday. Remind me about the dog’s medicine. Draft a note to the sitter.”

  2. 02

    It works on its computer

    Files, the web, the draft, and the reminder for next week happen on a computer of its own, so yours stays free for you.

    Day sheet. Grocery sheet. Sitter draft. Vet on the pet list.

  3. 03

    You approve what leaves

    Anything that would go out as you waits. You read it, edit a sentence, or let it go. The routine continues either way.

    The sitter note sits in drafts until you approve it.

Everyday

The kinds of hours it takes.

Ordinary errands and the quiet admin of a household. The same teammate, from the first cup of coffee to Sunday night.

6:40 am · Before coffee

The morning sheet

A one-page day is ready: the times, three habits, and the one thing that would make the afternoon easier. Print it, or leave it on the counter and get on with breakfast.

Thursday

  • Water

Lunch break

The choice you keep postponing

“Compare the three clinics within twenty minutes, and tell me which can see the dog on Thursday.” You get a short note. The tabs stay on its computer.

After pickup

The message you don’t want to write

A reply to the teacher, the landlord, or the group thread, in words that sound like you. It waits in drafts until you approve the send.

Sunday evening

The week, looked at plainly

A review of the plan: what you said you’d do, what happened, what to change. Schedule and the habit list have already compared notes.

Privacy

It asks before it speaks for you.

Consciousness drafts email and chat. It does not send them until you approve. The same pause holds whenever it would act in your name.

  • You read every outgoing message first.
  • Files stay in the places you already use: the sheet, the document, the Drive folder.
